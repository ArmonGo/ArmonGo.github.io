Postcards

A couple of charcoal-drawn postcards about topics that are close to me.

Started with the concept of trapping yourself inside a glass bottle: you can see the world outside, but you can’t break free.

I wrote a story about it; below are just a few paragraphs.

Thump, thump… Someone knocked heavily on my glass enclosure, yanking my curled-up consciousness out of sleep.

A crowd had gathered, packed with curious onlookers.

Suddenly, I found it hard to breathe—if I had a nose or a mouth, that is.

My drowsy eyes refused to cooperate, unwilling to open even a sliver. The harsh sunlight intruded like a thief breaking into my space, aggressively trying to pry my eyelids apart.

I didn’t need to look to know that today, the park must be overflowing with people.

Thump, thump… Another knock, this time with more persistence.

“This is bizarre. What is this thing made of? How is it so sturdy?”

I remained curled up on the ground, my eyelids not even twitching.

If possible, I would have liked to keep sleeping—a sleep without dreams.

Speaking of which, I’m not sure if that’s a blessing or a curse. I once overheard a tourist talking about dreams—those illusory yet vivid constructs of the mind, surreal worlds built within the confines of sleep.

“They can be the festering wounds of your psyche, or a frenzied carnival of ecstasy. Pain and joy, magnified a hundredfold, a thousandfold, flashing through you in a montage of sensations. Dreams are the closest thing to death, a space where you exist at the intersection of soul and consciousness.”

I have never dreamed. The ones trapped in glass bottles never dream. We do not know what it means to be alive, nor what it means to die.

Time, like stubborn limescale, has crusted over this singularity, unmoving. Existence and meaning have diverged, occupying every crevice of my awareness.

I stay in my bottle, day after day.

Or rather—perhaps “day after day” is not the right phrase, for there is no strict division of time here. But let me borrow the tourists’ perception of time and call each wave of visitors a “day.”

I stay in my bottle, day after day. I am neither bored nor resentful.

I just—

I just want to dream.

Tools: Procreate, postcards, charcoal.
